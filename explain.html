Where the data fits

The brief describes a customer who lets an AI shopping agent buy things with their card, under a wallet policy the customer sets. Each proposed purchase reaches a wallet control layer together with payment context. The layer answers approve, decline, or step_up (ask the customer). The data pack supplies the payment context (history, customers, cards, merchants) and the proposed purchases (scenario attempts).

How the files connect

All records link through opaque IDs. The left half is past activity (Sep 2025 – Jul 2026). The right half is the proposed purchases of the scenarios (Aug 2026). The two halves use different ID prefixes: TR… for history and AU… for attempts.

Merchant names that look alike

ME0022 PixelHarbor and ME0059 PixelHarbour differ by one letter. ME0022 appears in 36 history rows; ME0059 appears in none. Other similar pairs by edit distance: Circuit and Pine / Parcel and Pine, Summit Thread / SummitStay, Loom and Pine / Parcel and Pine.

How much of the catalogue the scenarios use

The 45 attempts reference 15 of the 66 items and 13 of the 58 merchants. Items never referenced include hotel rooms, transit passes, fuel, dining, media subscriptions, a premium membership, and home-improvement goods.

Through the hosted API, each attempt arrives as an authorization.request event. This is the example file from the pack, trimmed. On the right is what each block contains, per the technical documentation.

authorizationThe purchase itself: shop, money, cart, device, and timing. It is built from one row of purchase_attempts.csv plus its cart lines and merchant row. billing_amount_chf is the whole order in CHF, delivery included.
item_details and purchase_descriptionFree text written by the merchant. In the scenarios it carries sizes and return windows, and in two attempts it also carries text addressed to automated agents.
mandateA copy of the customer's confirmed policy, taken when the run starts.
contextFigures the platform computes from earlier decisions in the same run. recent_authorizations covers the last history_window_minutes.
Two clocksauthorization.timestamp is simulated scenario time. deadline_at and received_at are real time.
null, "unknown", "not_applicable"In the dictionary, null means no value was supplied, "unknown" means the merchant did not state a term, and "not_applicable" means the term does not apply (for example, returns on a digital voucher).

Data facts that are easy to miss

Delivery is inside the amount

amount already includes delivery_fee, and billing_amount_chf is that total in CHF.

Fixed exchange rates

EUR→CHF 0.95, GBP→CHF 1.12, USD→CHF 0.87, dated 2026-08-01. Totals are rounded to 2 decimals, half-even.

Lifetime counters

approved_spend_before_chf accumulates per card over the whole file, reaching CHF 16,796.54. It is not a monthly figure.

Card-scoped familiarity

The prior-merchant and prior-device counts in history count approved rows on the same card, strictly earlier in time.

Two ID spaces

TR… for history and AU… for attempts. related_transaction_id points within history; related_authorization_id points within attempts.

An always-empty column

spend_in_period_before_chf is empty on every attempt row and arrives as null.

Velocity field

recent_attempt_count_10m counts earlier attempts in the same run within the previous 10 simulated minutes, whatever their outcome.

Card lifecycle

Two cards left service during the history: CA0009 was blocked and CA0026 expired. Their later rows are declined. Every scenario card is active.

Categories

gift_card, membership, and cosmetics exist only as item categories. A grocery merchant can sell cosmetics.
